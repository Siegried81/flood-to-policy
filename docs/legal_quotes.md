## Directive 2007/60/EC — the Floods Directive

- **Full title:** Directive 2007/60/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 23 October 2007 on the assessment and management of flood risks.
- **CELEX:** 32007L0060 · **ELI (permanent):** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2007/60/oj · also http://data.europa.eu/eli/dir/2007/60/oj
- **In force:** 26 November 2007.

### Article 6(3) — the three hazard-map scenarios
> Flood hazard maps shall cover the geographical areas which could be flooded according to the following scenarios:
> (a) floods with a low probability, or extreme event scenarios;
> (b) floods with a medium probability (likely return period ≥ 100 years);
> (c) floods with a high probability, where appropriate.

**Caveat to state before a juror does:** a number is given for **(b) only**. Placing
RP10–RP75 as "high probability" and RP200–RP500 as "low probability / extreme" is
**this kit's reading**, not the Article's.

### Article 6(8) — hazard/risk maps deadline
> Member States shall ensure that the flood hazard maps and flood risk maps are completed by 22 December 2013.

### Article 7(2) — objectives of the flood risk management plans
> Member States shall establish appropriate objectives for the management of flood risks for the areas identified under Article 5(1) and the areas covered by Article 13(1)(b), focusing on the reduction of potential adverse consequences of flooding for human health, the environment, cultural heritage and economic activity, and, if considered appropriate, on non-structural initiatives and/or on the reduction of the likelihood of flooding.

**Policy hook:** the Directive itself favours **non-structural measures and reducing
the likelihood** of flooding — a textual basis for a "shift from dykes to prevention"
recommendation.

### Article 7(4) — the solidarity clause (upstream / downstream)
> In the interests of solidarity, flood risk management plans established in one Member State shall not include measures which, by their extent and impact, significantly increase flood risks upstream or downstream of other countries in the same river basin or sub-basin, unless these measures have been coordinated and an agreed solution has been found among the Member States concerned in the framework of Article 8.

### Article 7(5) — plans deadline
> Member States shall ensure that flood risk management plans are completed and published by 22 December 2015.

### Article 8(5) — the cross-border escalation mechanism
> Where a Member State identifies an issue which has an impact on the management of flood risks of its water and that issue cannot be resolved by that Member State, it may report the issue to the Commission and any other Member State concerned and may make recommendations as to how the issue should be resolved.
> The Commission shall respond to any report or recommendations from Member States within a period of six months.

**Used for:** the downstream-Member-State recommendation (e.g. the Netherlands on the Meuse).

### Article 14(3) — six-yearly review of the plans
> The flood risk management plan(s) shall be reviewed, and if necessary updated, including the components set out in part B of the Annex, by 22 December 2021 and every six years thereafter.

**Date discipline:** the Article's own words are **"22 December 2021 and every six years
thereafter."** The **22 December 2027** used in the brief is the *next* cycle (2021 + 6),
derived — not a date written in the text. Say it that way if asked.

### Article 14(4) — climate change in the reviews
> The likely impact of climate change on the occurrence of floods shall be taken into account in the reviews referred to in paragraphs 1 and 3.

**Used for:** the climate-projection point — the duty to account for climate change in reviews is in the text.

## Directive 2000/60/EC — the Water Framework Directive (cited by FD Art. 7(3))

- **CELEX:** 32000L0060 · **ELI:** https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2000/60/oj
- **Article 3** — river basin districts: the reporting unit the cross-border-basin figures are built on (the Floods Directive reuses them, its Article 3).
- **Article 4** — environmental objectives, which flood risk management plans must take into account under Floods Directive Art. 7(3).
